Three things a car has to do at once, scored together

Most vehicle comparisons pick one axis. This one holds three in tension: how much primary energy a vehicle burns per passenger-kilometre, whether it can restart on a steep wet concrete slope fully loaded, and what eight years of ownership costs under Central Java fuel prices and road tax. Forty-eight vehicles sold in Indonesia, scored on all three. Twenty-four of them cannot climb the hill at all, which settles their ranking before cost is considered.

        Where efficiency and resale strength overlap

        Screening the qualifying vehicles for both E ≤ 0.60 and a resale fraction ≥ 0.55 leaves a very short list, and its composition is the whole finding:

        Why 24 vehicles are excluded

        Not on cost, and not on efficiency. They cannot restart on the slope.

        At the current gradient and friction the traction ceiling is times the driven fraction. A vehicle needs an effective driven fraction above to reach M = 1.0. Weight transfers rearward on a slope in proportion to the gradient, so a front-drive vehicle's static 0.55 falls further still, while a rear-drive vehicle's 0.60 rises. That single mechanism, not engine output, decides most of these exclusions.

        How the three scores are built

        Every constant below is editable in the data block at the foot of this page.

        Formulas

        loaded mass      = curb + seats x 70 kg          (every seat occupied)
driven fraction  = static +/- (CG height / wheelbase) x gradient
                   front-drive loses, rear-drive gains, all-wheel unaffected
force required   = mass x g x (sin O + Crr x cos O)
force from torque= torque x ratio x driveline efficiency / tire radius
force from grip  = mu x mass x g x cos O x driven fraction
M                = min(torque, grip) / required        M < 1.0 cannot climb

E  petrol/diesel = (L/100km / 100 x kWh/L / well-to-tank) / occupancy
E  electric      = (Wh/km / 1000 / charge eff / grid eff) / occupancy
T                = (depreciation/km + energy/km + maintenance/km
                    + road tax/km + charger amortisation/km) / occupancy

score E = 100 x (1.00 - E) / 0.85                        clipped 0-100
score T = 100 x (5500 - T) / 5000                        clipped 0-100
score M = 50 + 50 x min((M - 1) / 0.5, 1)   if M >= 1.0
        = 0                                  if M <  1.0
Score   = (score E + score T + score M) / 3

        How much of this is measured

        Every input carries a source tier. Aggregate honestly: prices are solid, resale is not.

        Five independent research agents were asked to anchor resale values for the newer candidates against Indonesian used-car listings. None succeeded — partly because the question was badly framed (several of these models did not exist in Indonesia at the reference year), and partly because the listings do not exist in a comparable form. That gap is real and is marked T3 throughout rather than papered over.

        What would change the answer

        Ordered by how much damage each would do if wrong.

        Surface friction is the weakest number here

        No published source isolates the grip of algae-contaminated concrete. The 0.40 used as default matches the Transportation Research Board figure for clean wet Portland cement concrete, which means the model may be describing a surface with no biofilm at all. Real wet-plus-algae is plausibly 0.25 to 0.35. Drag the friction slider to see how fast the qualifying list collapses.

        Resale is mostly estimated, but barely matters

        Thirty-two of the original thirty-eight resale cells are T3 pattern estimates, and the newer candidates have no anchor at all. Testing a five-point resale collapse across all diesels moves scores by 0.6 to 0.9 points and changes four positions by one place each. Resale enters cost once, divided by 160,000 km and again by occupancy, and partly self-cancels because a lower valuation also lowers the road-tax base. Fuel price, paid on every kilometre, moves the ranking about twice as hard.

        Manufacturer consumption claims have been wrong by up to 66%

        One hybrid in this set is certified at 30 km/L and tested at 18.12 km/L in a controlled full-to-full run. Wherever a real-world figure exists it is used in preference to the claim, and tagged accordingly. Vehicles still carrying T3 consumption should be treated as optimistic.

        The electric vehicle tax exemption is a policy, not a constant

        Central Java exempts battery-electric vehicles from PKB and BBNKB under Kepgub 100.3.3.1/161 Tahun 2026, worth roughly Rp7/km against up to Rp697/km for a large petrol 4x4. National rules changed in April 2026 to make this a provincial discretion. It has already survived one challenge this year; it is not safe to assume it holds for eight.

        One row rests on a single source

        The Chery Omoda 5 GT is listed as all-wheel drive on the strength of one aggregator ranking and no corroboration. Drive layout decides whether a vehicle qualifies at all, so verify this at a dealer before relying on its position.

        Tires are not modelled, and they are the cheapest lever

        Friction is a property of road and tire together. A worn highway-terrain tire on wet contaminated concrete sits near 0.30; a fresh all-terrain or wet-grip tire on the same surface reaches 0.45 to 0.55. That difference is worth more than any drivetrain change in this table and costs about one percent as much.

        Frequently asked questions

        Answers grounded in the model above, not general advice.
        Which vehicle ranks first overall for a steep-hill driver in Central Java?

        The BYD Sealion 7 Performance AWD scores 81.4 of 100. It combines all-wheel drive (needed to clear a 24.89% wet-concrete grade), a tested 152 Wh/km, and exemption from PKB and BBNKB road tax in Central Java. Its weakness is resale: Indonesian battery-electric vehicles currently lose 40% or more in the first year, and the 0.27 eight-year resale fraction used here is a brand-pattern estimate, not measured.

        Why do the most efficient cars fail the ranking entirely?

        Because hill-start capability is a hard gate, not a weighted factor. At 24.89% grade on wet concrete (mu=0.4), every front-wheel-drive vehicle in the set produces M below 1.0 and cannot climb at all. The Wuling Aira EV has the best well-to-wheel efficiency in the entire set at 0.213 kWh per passenger-km, and it only just clears at M=1.000. Twenty-four of the forty-eight vehicles are disqualified on traction before cost or efficiency is considered.

        What is M, the hill-start margin?

        M is the ratio of available tractive force to the force required to hold and restart on the grade, fully loaded with every seat occupied. M below 1.0 means the vehicle cannot restart on the slope. The model takes the lower of two ceilings: torque at the contact patch, and the friction limit set by surface grip and the fraction of vehicle weight on driven wheels. On steep grades the friction limit usually binds first, which is why drive layout matters more than engine output.

        Why does rear-wheel drive climb better than front-wheel drive on a steep grade?

        Weight transfers rearward in proportion to the gradient. The model applies a load-transfer term of (CG height / wheelbase) times the grade. On a 24.89% slope a front-drive hatchback's effective driven fraction falls from 0.55 to about 0.498, while a rear-drive vehicle's rises from 0.60 to about 0.652. That swing of roughly 0.15 is the difference between failing and clearing. Toyota markets the rear-drive Innova Reborn on exactly this property.

        Can front-wheel drive climb a 24.89% wet concrete grade at all?

        No. A front-drive vehicle would need a friction coefficient of about 0.58 to reach M=1.0 at this grade, which is dry-concrete territory. On wet or algae-contaminated concrete no tire reaches that. Rear-wheel drive is marginal rather than impossible: it needs about 0.43, which fresh all-terrain or wet-grip tires can supply. All-wheel drive clears comfortably.

        Did the September 2026 fuel price rise change the ranking?

        Yes, and only for diesel. On 1 September 2026 Dexlite rose from Rp19,700 to Rp23,700 per litre (+20.3%) and Pertamina Dex from Rp21,150 to Rp25,200, while Pertamax held at Rp15,950, Pertalite at Rp10,000 and Biosolar at Rp6,800. Every diesel lost about 1.5 points and moved two to four places. After the adjustment no diesel remains in the top nine.

        Are electric vehicles genuinely cheaper to own in Central Java?

        On tax, decisively. Central Java exempts battery-electric vehicles from both PKB and BBNKB under Kepgub 100.3.3.1/161 Tahun 2026, so a BEV pays about Rp7 per km in statutory cost against Rp697 per km for a Ford Ranger Raptor. Electricity has also been held flat by policy for eight consecutive quarters while fuel repriced monthly. The offset is depreciation, which dominates cost of ownership for any vehicle above about Rp400 million and is where BEVs perform worst.

        Which is more volatile in this model, fuel price or resale value?

        Fuel, by roughly two to one. A single monthly Pertamina adjustment moved every diesel 1.5 points. A five-percentage-point resale collapse — a substantial market move — shifts scores by only 0.6 to 0.9 points and changes four positions by one place each. Energy cost is paid across all 160,000 km undiscounted; resale is a single terminal event divided by distance and occupancy, and it partly self-cancels because a lower valuation also lowers the road-tax base.

        What do the T1, T2 and T3 source tiers mean?

        T1 is a figure stated directly for that exact model by an authoritative source. T2 is derived by stated calculation from an authoritative source. T3 is a brand or class pattern estimate. In this dataset most prices are T1, most resale fractions are T3, and maintenance is weakest of all. Five independent verification agents were asked to anchor the newer candidates' resale values against Indonesian used listings and none succeeded.

        What is the least reliable number in this analysis?

        Surface friction. No published source isolates the grip of algae-contaminated concrete. The value used here, 0.40, matches the Transportation Research Board figure for clean wet Portland cement concrete — meaning the model may be describing wet concrete with no biofilm at all. Real wet-plus-algae is plausibly 0.25 to 0.35, which would lower the rear-wheel-drive crossing grade from 24.89% to somewhere near 21% or 18%.
